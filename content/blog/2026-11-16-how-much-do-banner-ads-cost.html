How much do banner ads cost? It depends on how they are sold. Auction-based display ads are priced per thousand impressions (CPM) or per click (CPC), so your total cost changes with competition, targeting and season. Flat-rate banner ads have a fixed price for a fixed run, such as $10 for 30 days, so you know the full cost before you start. This guide explains each pricing model, what is pushing prices up or down right now, and how to set a sensible budget for a small business.

The three ways banner ads are priced

Almost every banner ad you can buy falls into one of three pricing models. The price tag matters less than what you get for it, so compare the models side by side.

CPM: paying per thousand impressions

CPM means you pay a price for every 1,000 times your banner is shown. Most programmatic display ads, including the Google Display Network, run on auctions where the CPM changes with demand. Google also offers viewable CPM bidding, where you pay only for impressions that count as viewable. The industry standard from the Media Rating Council treats a display ad as viewable when at least 50% of its pixels are on screen for one continuous second.

The simple formula is: cost = (impressions / 1,000) x CPM. As an example, 200,000 impressions at a $5 CPM cost $1,000, and the same impressions at a $15 CPM cost $3,000. These are illustrations, not benchmarks; your real CPM depends on the audience, the sites and the time of year.

CPC: paying per click

With CPC pricing you pay only when someone clicks. That sounds safer, but display ads have low click-through rates because people are reading, not searching. A low click rate means the network has to show your ad many times to earn one click, so cost per click on display reflects both the CPM and how clickable your banner is. Flat rate: a fixed price for a fixed run

Flat-rate banner ads are sold like classified ads: you pick a spot and a period and pay one price. There is no bidding, no daily budget to watch and no surprise invoice. The trade-off is that you are not paying for a guaranteed number of impressions or clicks; you are paying for a placement. Why there is no single average banner ad cost

You will find many "average CPM" figures online, and they rarely agree. That is because they mix very different inventory: premium news sites, mobile apps, retargeting audiences, video and connected TV. A September 2026 budgeting guide from the agency Linear put it plainly: "There is no single reliable average across all display advertising" (Linear).

The main factors that move the price of a banner ad are:

  • Targeting. Narrow audiences, such as people who already visited your site, usually cost more per impression than broad audiences.
  • Placement quality. Prominent, above-the-fold spots on well-known sites cost more than placements lower on the page.
  • Banner size. In auctions, demand differs by format, so prices can differ too. Flat-rate sellers may charge the same for every size.
  • Season. More advertisers compete in the weeks before major shopping days, which pushes auction prices up.
  • Pricing model. Auction CPM and CPC prices change daily; flat rates do not.

What is happening to display ad prices in late 2026

Recent data shows how much auction prices can swing. AdRoll publishes a quarterly State of Digital Advertising Report based on performance data from more than 20,000 online businesses. Its Q3 2026 highlights report that from July 1 through September 8, display retargeting CPMs fell 29.1% and display prospecting CPMs fell 45.0% compared with the same period in 2025.

Earlier in the year the picture was different. In AdRoll's Q1 2026 report, covering January and February, retargeting CPMs rose 18% year over year while prospecting CPMs fell 11%, as PPC Land reported. In other words, the price of the same kind of impression moved by double digits within a few months.

Lower prices in late summer do not mean cheap ads in late November. AdRoll's report also notes that CPMs typically rise through early Q4 and peak near Black Friday and Cyber Monday. How much do banner ads cost for a small business budget?

For most small businesses the better question is not "what is the average CPM?" but "what can I afford to test, and how will I know if it worked?" Here is a simple way to set a banner advertising budget.

  1. Decide the goal. Awareness of a new shop, traffic to a sale page, or sign-ups for an event each need different measures of success.
  2. Set a test amount you can afford to lose. Treat the first month as a learning budget, not a guaranteed return.
  3. Choose the pricing model. If you have time to manage bids and targeting, CPM or CPC can scale. If you want a fixed cost and little upkeep, a flat-rate placement is easier to control.
  4. Tag every link. Add UTM parameters so each banner shows up separately in your analytics. Our guide to UTM parameters for banner ads walks through the setup.
  5. Judge cost per result, not cost per ad. Divide what you spent by the visits, leads or sales your analytics attribute to the banner.

A worked example

Here is an illustration with made-up numbers, not a forecast. A bakery spends $10 on a 30-day flat-rate banner. Its UTM-tagged link shows 25 visits in Google Analytics, and two visitors order a cake. The cost per visit is $0.40 and the cost per order is $5. If the same bakery ran an auction campaign and paid $40 for the same 25 visits, the cost per order would be $20. The comparison only works because both campaigns were tracked the same way.

CPM vs CPC vs flat rate: which pricing model to choose

Each model fits a different situation. Use this as a quick guide:

  • Choose CPM when you need to reach a specific audience at scale, have a budget of hundreds or thousands of dollars, and can monitor frequency and placements.
  • Choose CPC when site visits are the only goal and you are comfortable letting an algorithm decide where and how often your banner appears.
  • Choose flat rate when you want a predictable cost, a simple setup, and a steady presence in a placement you picked, and when your budget is small.

Many small businesses combine them: a flat-rate banner for steady awareness all month, plus a short auction campaign around a key date. Whatever you choose, keep the creative strong. A clear offer, a readable message and a matching landing page lower your cost per result under every pricing model.

Hidden costs to include in your banner ad budget

The media price is not the only cost. Before you compare offers, add up:

  • Design. Your time, a designer's fee, or a template tool to produce the banner in each size you need.
  • Minimum spends and fees. Some networks and agencies require a minimum budget or charge a management fee on top of media.
  • Your time. Auction campaigns need regular checks on placements, bids and frequency. Flat-rate ads need far less.
  • Landing page work. Sending clicks to a page that matches the ad often matters more than the ad price itself.

Frequently asked questions

How much do banner ads cost per month?

It depends on the pricing model. Auction-based display ads have no fixed monthly price; you set a budget and pay per impression or click. Flat-rate placements have a set monthly price that varies by site. On BannerPromote, any of the eight standard sizes costs $10 for 30 days.

What is a CPM in banner advertising?

CPM stands for cost per mille, or cost per thousand impressions. If a banner runs at a $5 CPM, 100,000 impressions cost $500. Some platforms, such as Google Ads, also offer viewable CPM, where you pay only for impressions that meet a viewability standard.

Is CPM or CPC cheaper for banner ads?

Neither is cheaper by default. CPC can look safer because you pay only for clicks, but the price per click reflects how often the network has to show your ad to earn one. Compare models by cost per visit or cost per sale in your own analytics.

Are cheap banner ads worth it?

They can be, if you treat them as a measured test. A low, fixed price lowers the risk of trying a new channel. Track the banner with a UTM-tagged link, give it the full run, and keep it only if the cost per visit or sale makes sense for your business.

Black Friday banner ads have one job: stop a shopper who is scrolling past dozens of offers and convince them that yours is worth a click. In 2026 that is harder than ever, because almost every business is advertising at once. This guide gives you twelve practical ideas for holiday banner ads, the sizes that work best, and a simple timeline so your campaign is live before the busiest shopping days of the year.

Why the 2026 holiday season matters for small businesses

Adobe Digital Insights expects U.S. shoppers to spend a record $275.1 billion online between November 1 and December 31, 2026, up 6.7% on last year. Cyber Week alone, from Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday, is forecast at $47.5 billion. Adobe expects Cyber Monday to be the biggest online shopping day of the season at $15.1 billion, with Black Friday growing even faster.

Two other numbers from the same Adobe holiday forecast report should shape your ads. Mobile devices are expected to drive a record 63% of online spending during Cyber Week, and traffic to retail sites from generative AI tools is forecast to rise about 130%. In plain terms: many of your shoppers will see your banner on a phone, and many will arrive already comparing prices. A small, clear, mobile-friendly banner with a specific offer beats a crowded design every time.

12 Black Friday banner ad ideas that get clicks

1. Lead with the number

"40% off everything" or "Save $25 today" is read in a fraction of a second. Make the discount the largest text in the banner and keep the rest short.

2. Show a real deadline

"Ends Monday midnight" creates honest urgency. Only use a deadline you will keep; shoppers notice when a "last chance" sale runs for weeks.

3. Feature one hero product

A single product photo on a clean background outperforms a collage. Pick your best seller or the item with the deepest discount.

4. Use a seasonal color, not a seasonal cliché

A touch of holiday color helps your ad feel timely, but avoid busy snowflake borders. Contrast between the button and background matters more than decoration.

5. Write a button that says what happens

"Shop the sale" or "Get 40% off" tells people exactly what the click does. Avoid vague buttons such as "Learn more".

6. Offer something besides a discount

Free shipping, a free gift with orders over a set amount, or gift wrapping can stand out when everyone else shouts percentages.

7. Target the gift buyer

"Gifts under $30" or "Perfect for dads" speaks to the person shopping for someone else, which is most holiday shoppers.

8. Run a Cyber Monday version

Swap the headline on Sunday night. A fresh "Cyber Monday: final hours" banner signals a new reason to click, even to people who saw your Black Friday ad.

9. Mention payment options

Adobe forecasts $21.3 billion in buy now, pay later spending during November and December. If you offer it, a small line such as "Pay in 4" can lower hesitation.

10. Keep animation short

An animated GIF can draw the eye, but end on a frame that makes sense on its own. Many people only see the last frame.

11. Match the landing page

Send clicks straight to the sale page or product, not your home page. If the banner says 40% off, the first thing visitors see should say 40% off too.

12. Plan your January follow-up

Returns, gift cards and "new year, new you" offers keep momentum after the holidays. Prepare a second banner now so you can switch on December 26.

Best banner sizes for holiday campaigns

Because so much holiday shopping happens on phones, start with sizes that work on every screen. The 300×250 medium rectangle fits desktop and mobile layouts. Add a 320×50 mobile banner for phone-only reach, and a 728×90 leaderboard to be the first thing desktop visitors see. Black Friday ad copy that converts

Good holiday ad copy is short and specific. Try this pattern: offer + product + deadline + action. For example, "30% off winter boots. Ends Monday. Shop now." Read your banner on a phone screen before you upload it; if you need to squint, cut words. Eye-tracking research from Nielsen Norman Group shows that people tend to skip anything that looks like a typical, cluttered ad, a habit known as banner blindness, so a clean design with one message has the best chance of being noticed. Keep your brand name or logo visible so even people who don't click remember who made the offer. Your Black Friday banner ad timeline

  1. Early November: finalize your offer and landing page, design two versions of your banner, and book your placement.
  2. Two weeks before Thanksgiving: go live with an early-access or "coming soon" message to capture early shoppers.
  3. Thanksgiving to Cyber Monday: run your main offer. Swap to the Cyber Monday banner on Sunday night.
  4. December: shift to last-shipping-date and gift-card messages.
  5. After December 25: run your January follow-up offer.

Black Friday banner ads: frequently asked questions

When should I start running Black Friday banner ads?

Have your ad live by mid-November. Shoppers compare deals well before Thanksgiving, and a 30-day run that starts in early November covers both Black Friday and Cyber Monday.

What is the best size for a holiday banner ad?

The 300×250 medium rectangle is the safest single choice because it works on desktop and mobile. Add a 320×50 mobile banner if most of your customers shop on phones.

Should I use a different banner for Cyber Monday?

Yes. A new headline such as "Cyber Monday: final hours" gives people who already saw your Black Friday ad a fresh reason to click.

How much text should a Black Friday banner have?

As little as possible: the offer, a deadline and a button. If someone can't read it in two seconds on a phone, it has too much text.

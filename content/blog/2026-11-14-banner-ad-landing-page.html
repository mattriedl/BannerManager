A banner ad landing page is the page people reach when they click your banner, and it decides whether that click becomes a sale, a booking or a lead. The best ones do one job: they repeat the promise the banner made, load quickly on a phone and make the next step obvious. This guide covers what to put on the page, how fast it needs to be, how to write it, how to measure it and a checklist to run before your ad goes live.

Why the landing page matters as much as the banner

The banner gets the click. What happens next depends on the page.

Google puts it plainly in its advice on optimizing ads and landing pages: when someone clicks an ad, "they expect to land on a page that's relevant to what they saw in your ad. If they don't immediately find what they expect, they're more likely to leave." That advice is written for search ads, but it applies just as well to a banner on a website.

This is also where small budgets win or lose. With a flat-price placement, you pay the same whether your page converts 1 visitor in 100 or 5 in 100. Improving the page is often the cheapest way to get more out of the same ad. What to put on a banner ad landing page

A good page answers three questions in the first screen: Am I in the right place? What do I get? What do I do now? Build it from these parts.

1. A headline that matches the banner

If the banner says "20% off winter boots," the page headline should say "20% off winter boots," not "Welcome to our store." Google's example makes the same point: if an ad promises shoes at 20% off, customers should be able to find and buy shoes at that price on the landing page. This is called message match, and it is the single most important fix for most small-business pages.

2. The same look as the ad

Use the colors, product photo or logo that appeared in the banner. Visual continuity tells visitors instantly that they followed the right link.

3. One clear call to action

Pick one action and make it the biggest button on the page: "Shop the sale," "Book a table," "Get a free quote." Google advises that your page "should also mirror the call-to-action in your ad text." If the banner said "Book now," the page should open with a booking button, not a menu.

4. The key details, near the top

Price, what is included, the deadline, delivery or service area. Google recommends putting important information toward the top of the page so visitors don't have to scroll to see it.

5. Reasons to trust you, and nothing in the way

Add genuine customer reviews, a phone number and a clear returns policy. Remove pop-ups that cover the offer and autoplay video; Google warns against cluttering a page with too many ads or pop-ups.

Speed and mobile: the part most small sites get wrong

Many banner clicks come from phones, often on a slow connection. Unbounce's 2024 Conversion Benchmark Report, based on more than 41,000 landing pages and 57 million conversions, found that mobile drove nearly five times more visitors than desktop, while desktop converted 8% better. In other words, most of your visitors are on the device where pages tend to perform worse.

How fast is fast enough?

Google's Core Web Vitals give three concrete targets for a good experience:

MetricWhat it measures"Good" target
Largest Contentful Paint (LCP)How quickly the main content appearsWithin 2.5 seconds
Interaction to Next Paint (INP)How quickly the page responds to taps200 milliseconds or less
Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS)How much the layout jumps while loading0.1 or less

You can test any page for free with Google's PageSpeed Insights. Paste in your landing page address and look at the mobile results first.

Why small speed gains matter

A Deloitte study summarized on web.dev, Milliseconds Make Millions, tracked 37 European and American brand sites and more than 30 million user sessions. After a 0.1-second improvement across four speed metrics, retail sites saw a 9.1% increase in visitors moving from product pages to the basket, and retail shoppers spent 9.2% more. The lesson for smaller sites: small delays cost real customers.

Quick speed fixes anyone can make

  • Compress the hero image and export it at the size it is displayed, not straight from the camera.
  • Remove chat widgets, sliders and tracking scripts you no longer use.
  • Give images fixed dimensions so the page doesn't jump while loading.
  • Make sure buttons are large enough to tap and the phone number is a tap-to-call link.

How to write landing page copy that converts

Short, plain words beat clever ones. Unbounce's 2024 report found that copy written at a 5th to 7th grade reading level converted at 11.1% across most industries it studied, compared with 7.1% for 8th and 9th grade copy and 5.3% for professional-level writing. The report also found that difficult words were the factor most strongly linked to lower conversion rates.

Rules that follow from that:

  • Lead with the benefit. "Warm feet all winter" before "Thinsulate-lined upper."
  • Use short sentences and short paragraphs. Visitors on phones scan; they don't read.
  • Cut jargon. Write the way you would explain the offer to a customer at the counter.
  • Be specific. "Order by December 15 for Christmas delivery" is clearer than "Order soon."

Landing page for display ads vs your home page

Pointing a banner at your home page is tempting, but it is usually the weaker choice.

Home pageDedicated landing page
FocusEverything you sellThe one offer in the banner
Message matchRarely matches the adRepeats the ad's headline and image
Next stepMany links competeOne clear button
MeasurementAd visits mix with all other trafficEasy to see what the ad produced

A dedicated page doesn't have to be new. An existing product page, a category page filtered to the sale or a booking page often works well, as long as it matches the banner.

Landing page checklist before your ad goes live

Run through this list on your own phone before you order.

  • The headline repeats the banner's offer word for word or close to it.
  • The price, deadline and main details are visible without scrolling.
  • There is one obvious button, and it does what it says.
  • The page loads quickly on mobile data, and PageSpeed Insights shows no major problems.
  • Nothing covers the offer: no pop-up on arrival, no cookie banner hiding the button.
  • The link includes your UTM tags and lands on the final page with no redirects.
  • The offer is still valid for the full 30 days the banner will run, or you have a plan to update it.

Frequently asked questions

Should a banner ad link to my home page or a landing page?

Usually a landing page. A page built around the banner's single offer, with matching headline and one button, gives visitors what they expected. A home page makes them search for it. An existing product or sale page works fine if it matches the ad.

What is a good landing page conversion rate?

It varies widely by industry, offer and traffic source, so compare against your own past results rather than a single number. Unbounce's 2024 data shows how much copy alone can matter: pages written at a 5th to 7th grade level converted at 11.1%, versus 5.3% for professional-level writing.

How fast should a landing page load?

Google's Core Web Vitals call a page good when its main content appears within 2.5 seconds, it responds to taps within 200 milliseconds and its layout barely shifts (a CLS of 0.1 or less). Test the mobile version with PageSpeed Insights.

Can I change my landing page after my banner ad is approved?

You can improve the page itself, such as faster images or clearer copy, as long as it stays the same address and follows the rules. At BannerPromote the link must not be redirected to another page or replaced with content that breaks the Terms of Service.

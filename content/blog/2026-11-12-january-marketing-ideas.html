The best January marketing ideas for small businesses do three things: they connect your product to the goals people set for the new year, they bring back holiday customers and gift card holders, and they take advantage of the quieter, cheaper advertising market that follows the holiday rush. January is often a slow month for sales, but it is also a month when many larger advertisers pull back. This guide explains why, then gives you practical ideas and a simple week-by-week plan for January 2027.

Why January is a good month to advertise

Many businesses treat January as a recovery month and cut their marketing. That is exactly why it can be a smart time to advertise.

Auction ad prices drop after the holidays

In auction-based advertising, prices rise when many advertisers compete for the same space and fall when they don't. December is the most competitive month of the year, and January is usually one of the least. In January 2026, overall U.S. programmatic CPMs (the cost per thousand ad views) fell 32.5% compared with December, and display CPMs fell 36.6%, according to DataBeat's Programmatic Trends Report as reported by PPC Land. Commenting on AdRoll data, MediaPost noted that "CPMs in January usually decline from December as the holiday season ends."

One reason is budgeting. Ad management company Mediavine explains that many advertisers plan spending by fiscal year, and when a new fiscal year starts in January, they spend cautiously at first.

Less noise means more attention

Lower prices are only part of the story. With fewer big brands running ads, your banner competes with fewer offers on the same page. If you buy placements at a flat rate rather than by auction, the price does not change by season, but the quieter environment still works in your favor.

People are making decisions

January is when people start memberships, book services they put off during the holidays and set budgets for the year. Businesses that are visible during that decision window are the ones that get considered.

What customers want in January

Good January advertising starts with what is on people's minds. In a YouGov poll published in December 2025, 31% of Americans said they would make a resolution or set a goal for 2026. Adults under 45 were about twice as likely as older adults to do so (43% vs. 21%). The most popular goals were:

You don't need to sell gym memberships to use these themes. A bookkeeper can speak to saving money, a restaurant to healthier menu options, a craft shop to a new hobby, and a home services company to finally fixing that project from last year. The point is to match your offer to a goal your customer already has.

10 January marketing ideas for small businesses

Pick two or three of these ideas rather than trying all of them. A focused message is easier to design, easier to measure and easier for customers to remember.

  1. Run a "fresh start" offer. Tie a discount or bundle to a specific new-year goal: "Start 2027 organized," "Your first month of lessons, 20% off." Name the goal in the headline.
  2. Welcome gift card holders. People who received your gift cards over the holidays are a warm audience. Promote what they can buy, and consider a small bonus for redeeming in January.
  3. Clear seasonal stock with a clear deadline. A clearance sale works best with a real end date and a specific discount, shown in the ad itself.
  4. Promote budget-friendly options. Saving money is a top goal. Highlight starter packages, payment plans or lower-priced alternatives instead of only your premium products.
  5. Book appointments for later in the quarter. Service businesses can fill February and March by advertising early-booking incentives in January.
  6. Bring back holiday customers. First-time holiday buyers are easy to lose. Use email and ads to invite them back with a reason to return, such as a new product or a loyalty reward.
  7. Launch something new. January is a natural moment for a new service, menu, class or product line. "New for 2027" is a simple, honest headline.
  8. Offer a free resource. A checklist, guide or free consultation tied to a new-year goal can generate leads when people are not yet ready to buy.
  9. Plan ahead for Valentine's Day. Valentine's Day 2027 falls on Sunday, February 14. Start teasing gifts, bookings or pre-orders in the second half of January.
  10. Stay visible on quiet weeks. Even a single, always-on banner keeps your name in front of customers while others pause, so you are remembered when spending picks up.

Common January marketing mistakes

  • Going silent. Stopping all marketing on January 1 means starting from zero when customers return. A small, steady presence is cheaper than rebuilding attention later.
  • Leaving holiday ads running. Old shipping deadlines and "last chance for Christmas" messages make a business look inactive. Swap creative as soon as the season ends.
  • Discounting everything. Deep, store-wide sales can train customers to wait. Use targeted offers tied to a goal, a product line or a deadline.
  • Vague resolution messages. "New year, new you" appears everywhere. Name the specific result your customer wants, such as "organize your garage in one weekend."
  • Not measuring. Without tagged links, you can't tell which offer worked, and you will be guessing again next January.

Frequently asked questions

Is January a good time to advertise a small business?

Often, yes. Auction-based ad prices usually fall after the holidays because fewer advertisers are competing. In January 2026, U.S. programmatic display CPMs fell 36.6% from December, according to DataBeat data reported by PPC Land. With fewer competing ads, a clear offer can be easier to notice.

Why are ad costs lower in January?

Demand drops. Many brands spend heavily in November and December, then pull back when a new budget year starts in January. In auction-based advertising, less competition means lower prices. Flat-rate placements cost the same all year, but they still benefit from a less crowded page.

What should a small business promote in January?

Offers tied to new-year goals (health, saving money, organization, learning something new), gift card redemption, clearance of seasonal stock, early bookings for later in the quarter, and new products or services launched for the new year.

When should I start planning January marketing?

In November or early December. Holiday weeks are busy, so design your January ads, landing pages and tracking links before the rush, and schedule placements so they start in the first week of January.

How do I beat the post-holiday sales slump?

Give customers a new reason to buy: a fresh-start offer, a gift card incentive or a new product. Keep your business visible with low-cost, always-on ads, and invite holiday customers back with a specific reason to return.

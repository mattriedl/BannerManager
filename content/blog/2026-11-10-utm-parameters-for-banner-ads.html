Key takeaways

  • UTM parameters are short tags added to the end of your banner's link so your analytics tool can tell which ad, site and campaign sent each visitor.
  • Always set utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign; add utm_content to tell banner sizes or versions apart.
  • Use utm_medium=banner or display so Google Analytics 4 groups the visits under its Display channel.

  • Tag your holiday banners before Black Friday, so the busiest weeks of the year are measured from the first click.

UTM parameters for banner ads are labels you add to the end of the link behind your banner, such as ?utm_source=bannerpromote&utm_medium=banner&utm_campaign=holiday-sale. When someone clicks the ad, your analytics tool reads those labels and records exactly where the visit came from. Without them, banner clicks often blend into generic "referral" traffic, and you can't tell whether the ad paid for itself. This guide shows which tags to use, how to name them, how to build a tagged link in a few minutes and where to read the results in Google Analytics 4 (GA4).

What UTM parameters are and why banner ads need them

A UTM parameter is a key and value pair added after a question mark in a URL. The visitor still lands on the same page; the tags are just extra information your analytics tool records with the visit.

Banner ads especially benefit from tagging for three reasons:

  • You may run the same offer in several places. Tags separate a banner on one site from a newsletter link or a social post that points to the same page.
  • You may run several sizes or designs. Tags show which creative earned the click.
  • Your ad seller may not give you reports. BannerPromote, for example, sells flat-rate placements and does not provide a reporting dashboard or promise a number of clicks. Your own analytics, fed by UTM tags, is where you judge results.

The tags work with any analytics tool that reads them, including GA4 and most privacy-focused alternatives. The examples below use GA4 because it is the most common.

How to set up UTM parameters for banner ads

Google's help page on collecting campaign data with custom URLs lists the parameters GA4 understands. It says you "should always use utm_source, utm_medium, and utm_campaign." Here is how each one maps to a banner campaign:

ParameterWhat it recordsBanner ad example
utm_sourceThe site or vendor that sent the visitbannerpromote
utm_mediumThe type of marketingbanner
utm_campaignThe promotion or offerholiday-sale-2026
utm_contentThe specific creative or version300x250-giftcards
utm_id (optional)A campaign ID, if you keep a list of campaignshs26

Google describes utm_content as the tag to "differentiate creatives," which makes it the natural place to record banner size or design. utm_term is meant for paid search keywords, so you can leave it off banner links. Google also lists utm_creative_format and utm_marketing_tactic, but notes that neither is currently reported in Google Analytics properties, so they add little for a small advertiser.

Why utm_medium=banner matters in GA4

GA4 sorts visits into default channels such as Organic Search, Referral and Display. According to Google's traffic-source dimensions reference, manually tagged traffic lands in the Display channel when the medium is one of "display", "banner", "expandable", "interstitial" or "cpm". If you invent your own medium, such as web-ad, GA4 may file the visits under a different channel or leave them as Unassigned. Use banner or display and your banner visits will sit together in the Display row.

Step by step: build a tagged banner link

  1. Start with the right landing page. Link to the page that matches the ad's offer, not your home page. For example, https://www.example.com/gift-cards.
  2. Open a URL builder. Google's free Campaign URL Builder fills in the format for you. A spreadsheet works too once you know the pattern.
  3. Fill in source, medium and campaign, then add content for the size or version.
  4. Copy the finished link. It will look like this:
    https://www.example.com/gift-cards?utm_source=bannerpromote&utm_medium=banner&utm_campaign=holiday-sale-2026&utm_content=300x250-giftcards
  5. Test it. Open the link in a private browser window, then check GA4's Realtime report. You should see the visit with your source and campaign within a minute or two.
  6. Use it as your ad's destination. On BannerPromote you enter the tagged address as your banner's link when you create your ad. Our guide on how to buy banner ads covers the rest of the setup.

If your landing page URL already contains a question mark, for example /shop?category=gifts, join the UTM tags with & instead of a second question mark.

A UTM naming system that keeps reports clean

Google's help page warns that parameter values are case sensitive: "utm_source=google is different from utm_source=Google." One stray capital letter splits your results into two rows. A few simple rules prevent that:

  • Use lowercase only. holiday-sale, never Holiday-Sale.
  • Pick one separator. Hyphens or underscores, not both. Avoid spaces, which turn into %20 in links.
  • Keep source and medium fixed. The same vendor should always get the same source name. The medium for every banner should always be banner.
  • Put the date or season in the campaign. holiday-sale-2026 won't collide with next year's sale.
  • Use content for size and version. Something like 728x90-v1 or 320x50-freeshipping lets you compare creatives. See our banner ad sizes guide for the eight standard sizes.
  • Write it down. A one-tab spreadsheet with columns for date, landing page, each tag and the final link stops guesswork when you build the next campaign.

How to track banner ad clicks and sales in GA4

Once tagged visits start arriving, these are the places to look:

  • Traffic acquisition report. In GA4, go to Reports, then Acquisition, then Traffic acquisition. Change the primary dimension to Session source / medium and look for bannerpromote / banner. Switch the dimension to Session campaign to compare promotions.
  • Add a secondary dimension. Add Session manual ad content to see results by utm_content, meaning by size or design.
  • Key events. Mark the actions that matter, such as a purchase, a booking request or a contact form submission, as key events. The same report then shows how many came from each banner.

To judge a flat-rate banner, divide what you paid by the result you care about. For example, a $10 placement that brings two bookings costs $5 per booking. That is a better measure than click-through rate alone, which our article on banner ad click-through rate explains in more detail.

Expect some gaps. Ad blockers, cookie consent choices and visitors who come back later by typing your address can all hide part of a banner's effect. Treat tagged numbers as a reliable floor, not the full total.

Common UTM mistakes that break banner ad tracking

  • Tagging internal links. Don't add UTM tags to links between pages on your own site. They can start a new session and overwrite the real source of the visit.
  • Redirects that drop the tags. Some site setups redirect example.com to www.example.com or HTTP to HTTPS and lose everything after the question mark. Link straight to the final address and test it.
  • Mixed spellings. bannerpromote, banner-promote and BannerPromote become three sources. Copy from your spreadsheet rather than retyping.
  • Personal information in tags. Never put email addresses, names or phone numbers in a URL. Analytics tools and site logs store them.
  • Changing the link mid-campaign. If you need a new version, give it a new utm_content value so the old and new results stay separate.

Frequently asked questions

Do UTM parameters affect SEO?

Not when used on ad links. UTM tags on links from other sites only tell your analytics where a visit came from. Avoid adding them to your own internal links, and keep a canonical tag on your pages so search engines treat the tagged and untagged addresses as the same page.

What should utm_medium be for banner ads?

Use banner or display. Google Analytics 4 places manually tagged traffic with either medium in its Display channel, so all your banner visits are grouped together.

Which UTM parameters are required?

Google recommends always using utm_source, utm_medium and utm_campaign. For banner ads, adding utm_content is worth it so you can compare sizes and designs.

Why aren't my UTM-tagged banner clicks showing in Google Analytics?

Check that the tags survive any redirects, that the spelling and capitalization match what you expect, and that your GA4 tag fires on the landing page. Test the link in a private window and watch the Realtime report.

Can I track banner ad conversions without Google Analytics?

Yes. Most analytics tools and many ecommerce platforms read UTM tags. You can also use a unique discount code per banner as a second check on sales.

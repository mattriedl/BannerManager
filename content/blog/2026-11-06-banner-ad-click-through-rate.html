Key takeaways

  • Display and banner ads typically see click-through rates around 0.1%, far below search ads, because people aren't actively searching when they see them.
  • Your banner ad click-through rate is only one signal; what matters is the cost per visit and what visitors do next.
  • Clear offers, clean design, mobile-friendly sizes and matching landing pages are the biggest levers.

Your banner ad click-through rate (CTR) is the share of people who click your ad after seeing it. It is one of the first numbers advertisers look at, and one of the most misunderstood. A CTR that would be a disaster for a search ad can be perfectly healthy for a banner. This guide explains what a good banner ad CTR looks like, why it is lower than other channels, and ten ways to improve yours.

How to calculate click-through rate

CTR is clicks divided by impressions (the number of times the ad was shown), multiplied by 100. If your banner was shown 10,000 times and clicked 15 times, your CTR is 15 ÷ 10,000 × 100 = 0.15%.

What is a good banner ad click-through rate?

Industry figures put the average display ad CTR at around 0.1%, based on data from Bannerflow's ad platform. Results vary widely by industry, ad size, placement and creative, so treat that as a rough baseline rather than a target. As a rule of thumb, a display banner performing consistently above 0.1% is doing well, and anything several times higher is excellent.

Compare that with search ads. WordStream's 2026 benchmarks put the average Google search ad CTR at 6.64%. The difference isn't because banners are bad; it is because of intent. Search ads appear to someone actively looking for an answer. Banner ads appear while someone is reading something else, so most impressions build awareness rather than clicks.

Why banner ad CTR is lower than other channels

The main reason is banner blindness: people learn to ignore anything that looks like an ad. Eye-tracking research from Nielsen Norman Group has found this behavior repeatedly since the late 1990s. Visitors skip areas where ads usually sit, ignore elements with ad-like styling such as flashy formatting and animation, and even overlook useful content placed next to ads.

That doesn't make banners pointless. It means a banner has to earn its click with a clear, relevant message, and that the value of a campaign includes the many people who see your brand without clicking.

Why CTR isn't the whole story

A high CTR with an expensive click can be worse than a modest CTR with a cheap one. With flat-rate pricing, every extra click lowers your cost per visit, but the number that pays the bills is what happens after the click: sign-ups, bookings and sales. Track all three with UTM-tagged links and judge a banner by cost per result, not CTR alone.

Imagine a flat-rate banner that costs $10 for a month and is shown 10,000 times. At a 0.1% CTR it brings 10 visits, which is $1.00 per visit. Improve the design so the CTR doubles to 0.2% and the same $10 brings 20 visits, or $0.50 each. On an auction platform where clicks cost a few dollars each, that same $10 might buy only two or three visits.

Now add conversions. If 1 in 20 visitors buys something worth $40, the 20-visit banner produces one sale and has paid for itself four times over, while the 10-visit version only breaks even on average. That is why improving CTR is worth the effort, and why you should always measure sales, not just clicks.

A quick CTR checklist before you upload

  • Can you read the headline in two seconds on a phone?
  • Is there exactly one offer and one button?
  • Does the landing page repeat the offer in its first line?
  • Is your link tagged so you can see the visits in your analytics?

10 ways to improve your banner ad click-through rate

  1. Lead with one specific benefit. "Free delivery this week" beats "Quality products since 1998".
  2. Use a button that states the action. "Get a free quote" tells people what the click does.
  3. Cut the words. If it takes longer than two seconds to read, most people won't.
  4. Make it readable on a phone. Large type and high contrast; test at real size before uploading.
  5. Choose a size that gets seen. The 300×250 medium rectangle and 300×600 half page are prominent and fit many layouts. Compare them in our banner ad size guide.
  6. Look clean, not cluttered. Simple layouts avoid the "this is an ad, skip it" reaction.
  7. Use animation sparingly. A short animation can attract attention; constant motion annoys. End on a frame that works alone.
  8. Be relevant to the page. Ads that suit the surrounding content are more likely to be clicked.
  9. Refresh your creative. People stop noticing the same banner over time. Swap in a new version every month or two.
  10. Test two versions. Change one thing, such as the headline or image, and keep the winner.

Banner ad click-through rate: frequently asked questions

What is the average click-through rate for banner ads?

Industry data puts the average display ad CTR at around 0.1%, though it varies by industry, size, placement and design.

Why is my banner ad CTR so low?

Most people seeing a banner aren't actively looking for what you offer, and many have learned to ignore ads. A clearer offer, a cleaner design and a more relevant placement usually help.

Is a 1% CTR good for a banner ad?

Yes. For display advertising, a 1% click-through rate is far above the typical average of around 0.1%.

Which banner ad size gets the most clicks?

Larger, prominent sizes such as the 300×250 medium rectangle and 300×600 half page often perform well, but placement and creative matter more than size alone.
